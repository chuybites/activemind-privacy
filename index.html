Privacy Policy

ActiveMind · Last updated: November 2026

The short version

You don't need an account to use ActiveMind. What you log (moods, journal, check-ins, your streak) stays on your iPhone, and in your own iCloud if you keep backup on. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

ActiveMind has one small server, and it is only for things between people: sharing a day trip as a link, and a circle of friends. It is used only if you choose to do one of those things. It never holds your moods, journal or places. From Health it holds only the few numbers you switch on for your circle, one switch each, off until you turn it on.

For things like the weather, places, recipes and the night sky, the app asks public services. Each one is named below with exactly what goes to it: never your name, and where a place is needed, a rounded spot. Anything that uses the camera, the microphone or Apple's on-device tools is worked out on your iPhone and stays there.

What stays on your iPhone

Your name and goals, mood check-ins, journal entries, activities and trips you finish, your streak, your routines, weekly goals and lifts, the foods you looked up and the meals you logged, your shopping list, your sleep program's diary, the flights you add, your sky log and your settings are stored on your iPhone. If you delete the app, they are removed from the phone.

iCloud backup

On unless you turn it off in Settings. Everything you keep in ActiveMind (check-ins, journal, activities, goals, trips, places and settings) is backed up to your own iCloud as you leave the app, only what changed, at most every 15 minutes, each phone's apart, so a new phone can bring it all back. Your writing in it is encrypted with your iCloud Keychain's keys. Apple stores it in your account; ActiveMind never sees it. Delete all my data removes it.

Apple Health

Only with your permission, which you can change any time in Settings › Health › ActiveMind.

  • Reads: your activity (steps, distance, energy, exercise, stand hours, flights, and workouts with their routes, effort ratings, Cardio Recovery and the running, swimming and cycling readings saved with them), heart rate, resting heart rate and HRV, sleep, breathing rate, breathing disturbances during sleep and blood oxygen, wrist temperature, walking steadiness and gait, VO2 max, time in daylight, past mindful minutes and moods, caffeine and water other apps saved (asked for the first time you log a drink or a glass), meals and nutrients other apps saved and alcoholic drinks logged in Health (shown as what was logged, never as a target), the sound levels around you from the Watch and in your headphones, and your date of birth (for fitness age, your heart-rate zones and your birthday; asked only if you connect Health in onboarding or Settings).
  • Reads, only if you say yes when Health asks: your cycle days, to see how your check-ins sit around your period; and, from iOS 26, only the medicines you pick in Health's own sheet and the doses you log for them, to count your check-ins in the four weeks before and after a start, a stop or a new amount. Neither leaves your iPhone, goes to friends or into points; both are hidden with your journal while it is locked, and reach the summary for your doctor only if you switch them on there. ActiveMind never advises on a medicine.
  • Writes: Mindful Minutes when you finish a check-in, a session, a meditation or an activity; State of Mind when you log how you feel (including through Siri, a widget or your Watch); caffeine when you log a coffee or another drink, and water when you log a glass (taking one back removes it); a workout's effort rating when you give one; a home workout you finish, as strength training or flexibility, with its time and no calories; and, on iOS 26 and later, a walk, run, hike or ride you start from an activity's timer, recorded on your iPhone with its distance, energy and route.
  • The worry and low mood checks: only while Share with the Health app is on (it is off until you turn it on), the checks Health keeps from its own questionnaires or another app are read, and the ones you take in ActiveMind are saved to Health with every answer. Turning it off stops both ways; what is already in Health stays there for you to delete.

Health data is used to show you your own readings in the app. Only if you have a circle and turn on a switch for it in the circle's "What you share", one number a day of that kind goes to ActiveMind's server for your friends' points and challenges: your step total, one sleep score a night (never its stages), or your active minutes. Turn a switch off and what it shared is removed. Steps typed into Health by hand never count. Nothing else from Health (heart rate, HRV, stages, breathing, temperature, weight, moods) ever goes to ActiveMind's server or to anyone else, and none of it is used for advertising.

Location

Used for weather, warnings, nearby places and day trips while the app is open. Three things can use it in the background:

  • Severe weather and Storm heads-up notifications, while they are on in Settings › Notifications: a check iOS runs now and then sends where you last were, rounded to about a kilometre, to the National Weather Service.
  • Tick stops as I arrive, when you turn it on for a day trip: iOS watches a circle round each of that day's stops, even while ActiveMind is closed, and tells the app only when you reach one. It needs Location set to Always. Only that day's stops are watched, and where you are never leaves your iPhone for it.
  • A walk, run, hike or ride you record from its timer: where you go is read while it records (the blue location pill shows) and kept only as that workout's route in Apple Health.

Your spot is sent, with nothing else attached, to the services that answer:

  • Open-Meteo for weather, tonight's sunset sky, summer dust, the last 30 years of highs and lows here (rounded to about ten miles), the height of the ground on the streets around you for Hill Repeats, and, for an outdoor workout another app recorded without the weather, where it began (rounded to about a kilometre) and its day
  • The National Weather Service for weather warnings, heat stress, the chance of thunder and beach forecasts, in the United States, rounded to about a kilometre; and for radar pictures of a square of the map from a grid everyone shares, several hundred miles wide until you zoom in, so where you are is never sent for the radar
  • Apple's WeatherKit for the next hour's rain, rounded to about a kilometre, at most every 10 minutes; the Apple Weather mark shown beside it comes from Apple too
  • The EPA's AirNow for air quality, in the United States, rounded to about seven miles
  • OpenStreetMap for opening hours, nearby courts, restrooms, water, parking, markets, picnic spots, public art, gardens and trails, and streets with a climb or public steps
  • The National Park Service for parks, their ranger programs and things to do
  • Wikipedia for what a place is, and Wikidata for when it was built and any historic listing (the entry's id only, found through that article)
  • iNaturalist for what people have seen near a park or within five miles of you
  • 7Timer!, a free astronomy forecast, for how steady and clear tonight's air will be, rounded to about a kilometre
  • Ticketmaster for events within 25 miles, the spot as a box about three miles wide
  • The USDA's Local Food Directories for farmers markets within ten miles, rounded to about a kilometre
  • Florida's evacuation zone map, only while a storm is near or when you add your home in Settings: a box about three miles wide round where you are, or your home, and your iPhone works out the zone. The home you add stays on your iPhone and is not in the iCloud backup
  • The US Department of Energy's list of charging stations, only with I drive electric on: the line of a trip's longest drive, with both ends cut off and rounded to about 100 metres so it does not name your door, and your plug type
  • Apple Maps, which draws the maps (including the ones on a trip's share card and the map a trip keeps for when there is no signal), gives the drive time to a trip's next stop with today's traffic, the walking routes of a loop, how long by bus or train to a place you open, Look Around and a place's own card, the town at each place in your passport (rounded to about a hundred metres), the county of a Florida restaurant, and finds an address or city you type yourself (your home, a flight's city)

Kept on your iPhone: where today's trips were planned from, the places you finish trips at, and the routes of your walks, runs and hikes from Apple Health, which together draw your Discovery map. The routes never go on its share card or to friends, and a switch on the map hides them.

Services asked with nothing about you

These are asked a question that says nothing about who or where you are (the same on every phone, or only a topic you picked), or hand over one public list that your iPhone reads for itself:

  • The sky and space: NOAA's space-weather forecast (the aurora), The Space Devs' Launch Library (rocket launches from the Cape and how many people are in space), NASA (today's asteroid passes) and CelesTrak (the space station's orbit, once a day). When something passes over you, and which way to look, is worked out on your iPhone.
  • The water: NOAA's tides (your iPhone picks the nearest station, so NOAA learns only which station), NOAA's buoys (one list of every buoy's latest reading), and in Florida FWC's red tide samples (its whole list of the last eight days).
  • Storms: the National Hurricane Center's list of active storms and each storm's forecast. Whether one could reach you is worked out on your iPhone.
  • Getting around: the public lists of bikes and scooters for hire that a city's companies publish (counted on your iPhone, and only asked when you are inside the area a company works in), and Queue-Times for the waits at a theme park's rides (the park's id only).
  • Food: the FDA's and the USDA's public recall lists, read whole once a day while you have looked up a packaged food in the last 90 days and compared with it on your iPhone; and, for a restaurant in Florida, the State's public file of that district's restaurant inspections, read at most once a week.
  • An evening in: TMDB for films on each of the fourteen streaming services the app lists (the same questions whichever services you have, so which ones you have is never sent), Radio Browser for stations by one word such as jazz (playing one connects your iPhone to that station's own stream, and the directory is told the station was played), the Open Trivia Database for questions, and Lichess for the chess puzzle of the day.
  • Reading and listening: Apple's podcast catalogue for the newest episodes of a few shows on a topic you pick, the Internet Archive for LibriVox's books read aloud, Open Library for well-loved books on a topic, the Wikibooks Cookbook for a recipe's page, the Art Institute of Chicago for a public-domain painting to draw from, Good News Network and Positive News for a good-news headline, and the news sites you choose to follow, asked for their own feed as a browser would.
  • ActiveMind's own switches: on each open the app reads a short list of on-and-off switches from ActiveMind's server, so an outside service that breaks can be paused without an update.

The forests, refuges and campgrounds on Days out, and everyday foods' numbers from the USDA, are lists inside the app, so finding them asks nobody.

Services that get something you typed or picked

  • MedlinePlus, the US National Library of Medicine's health library: the words you search, or the topic a page names
  • The USDA's FoodData Central: the words you type when you search packaged foods by name
  • Open Food Facts, a free and open database of packaged foods: only a barcode's number. The camera reads the barcode on your iPhone and keeps no picture
  • The Wikibooks Cookbook, an open cookbook: the words of a recipe search you type
  • Spoonacular: a restaurant chain's name, for its menu, and the words of a recipe search the open cookbook could not answer
  • xeno-canto, a community library of bird recordings: the scientific name of a bird whose call you tap to hear
  • Wikivoyage, the open travel guide: the name of a town a day out goes to, and where that town is
  • Uber and Lyft: their buttons open those apps or sites with only the place to go to filled in. Where you are is never sent by ActiveMind; the ride app asks you itself
  • Pages that open in Safari go to their own sites and follow their own policies: a ticket page, a film's page, the PAR-Q+ safety check (what you answer there goes to that site, not to ActiveMind) and FindTreatment.gov for low-cost counseling (you type where you are on their page yourself)

The camera, the microphone and Apple's on-device tools

Each of these is worked out on your iPhone. What the camera sees, what the microphone hears and how you move are never recorded, kept or sent; only what is named here is kept:

  • A barcode or a Nutrition Facts label: read by the camera on your iPhone; only the label's numbers are kept, with that barcode
  • A paper page brought into the journal: a photo from the camera, or one you pick from Photos, is read for its words by Apple's text recognition. Only the words are kept, as an entry you look over first; ActiveMind sees only the photos you pick
  • Talking to write (from iOS 26): what you say is turned into words by Apple's speech-to-text on your iPhone and joins the box for you to fix before saving
  • The camera as a coach, in a home workout or a yoga activity: Apple's Vision finds your joints in each picture, about ten a second, and the picture is dropped at once. Only the reps it counted are kept
  • Listen tonight, on the sleep page, with the phone on its charger: Apple's sound recognizer listens through the night and the app keeps only counts a minute, how much was snoring and how many coughs. iOS shows the orange dot while it listens
  • AirPods counting: with AirPods in, a home workout reads how your head moves to count reps; only the counts are kept
  • Apple Intelligence, on a phone that has it: Apple's on-device model reads the last 30 days of your journal entries and check-in notes to write three short lines about your month, and reads a meal you say in a sentence. A friend's messages are never read, and a month with a note about hurting yourself is not summed up
  • Names in your notes: the people and places in your notes are found with Apple's language tools, for Mind's Came up this month; they are never sent or stored anywhere
  • Journaling suggestions: when you tap From your day, Apple's own sheet shows moments from your phone and ActiveMind is handed only the one you pick. It keeps words from it as the start of an entry and never opens its photos
  • Your own voice: with Read in my voice on, stories and poems are read in the Personal Voice you made in iOS. ActiveMind is told only yes or no when it asks to use it
  • Contacts: only if you say yes when an activity offers to show whose birthday is next. The names and birthdays in your contacts are read on your iPhone each time; nothing from your contacts is kept or sent

Other things on your iPhone that ActiveMind can use

  • Screen Time, for the detox hour: only when you choose apps to pause on a phone-free activity. iOS asks once, you pick the apps in Apple's own list, and Apple's Screen Time pauses them for the activity's time and opens them again when it ends. ActiveMind holds only Apple's sealed tokens for your choice, on your iPhone, and never learns which apps they are.
  • Apple Music: only if you pick a playlist under an activity's timer. Apple asks once, then the playlist plays, full songs with your own subscription or previews without one. ActiveMind never changes your library, and keeps only which playlist you picked, on your iPhone.
  • Reminders: when you send your shopping list, ActiveMind adds what your recipes need to a list in Reminders and reads that list only to leave out what is already on it.
  • Alarms: the sleep program's wake and nap alarms go through Apple's AlarmKit on iOS 26, which asks you once; before iOS 26 they are notifications. The program's diary and your answers stay on your iPhone.
  • Your Apple Watch: with the ActiveMind app on your Watch, your iPhone hands it today's readiness, sleep, steps, the day's three picks and the check-in's faces, and a check-in or a finished pick comes back. They go only between your own iPhone and Watch. On your Watch opens Apple's own sheet to put a workout in the Watch's Workout app.
  • Together on FaceTime: only when you start it on a FaceTime call, or join one someone started. A breathing session or an activity's timer then runs in step on everyone's phone through Apple's SharePlay. What goes to the others on the call is where it is (how far in, paused or not, the breathing pattern or the activity's name), through Apple, and nothing is kept.

Your account (optional)

Only needed to share a trip as a link or to have a circle of friends. You sign in with Apple; there is no password. ActiveMind's server, run on Supabase in the United States, then keeps:

  • the sign-in identifier Apple gives it
  • an email address from Apple, which is a private relay address if you choose Hide My Email
  • the name and emoji you pick
  • if you add friends, your profile card as you fill it in: a colour and pattern, a second emoji, a title, up to three tags, three short answers, three badges and three numbers you choose (such as your streak), four favourites and a short line about what you are up to on its back, one thing you showcase (a record, a crown, a day trip or a badge; a record carries its number only when that reading is switched on under What you share), and how the front is laid out; each part can be kept to yourself
  • if you make a face in the app and choose to wear it instead of your emoji, that face as a short code (its skin tone, hairstyle, hair colour, eyes, mouth, and whether it has glasses or a beard), so your friends see it on your card; switching back to your emoji removes it
  • if you allow notifications from your circle, your iPhone's push address, whether friend notifications are on, your quiet hours and your time zone, so a message or invite can reach you; removed when you sign out

Nothing you log goes there. You can delete your account in Settings › Account › Delete my account, which removes it and everything linked to it (trip links, your profile card, your circle and everything you sent friends) from the server. What is on your iPhone stays.

Trip links

Only when you send one. The day goes to ActiveMind's server as you see it: its stops, times, addresses and map, with your name and emoji. Where you planned it from is left off. Anyone with the link can see that one day, and nothing else of yours, on a web page and in the app, for 30 days or until you stop it in Settings › Account › Trip links. The web page shows the map using OpenStreetMap.

Your circle

Only if you add friends, up to five, each with an invite link. Your friends see your profile card, a status you set (for a day), your streak, which days you moved in the last 90 (an activity done, a day trip finished, or 5,000 steps), the cheers, notes, plans, postcards and pokes you send them, and your answer to the week's question once they have answered too. Friends can see when you've read their messages, unless you turn Read receipts off in Circle settings; then they see only that it arrived. A postcard carries a finished day trip's name, stops and date, never where you set out from. Never your moods, journal or places. Messages are cleared after 60 days, and moved days after 90. Either of you can remove the other at any time, which removes what you sent each other.

Your birthday: only if you turn on Share my birthday in Circle settings. Its month and day, never the year, goes on your profile on ActiveMind's server, where only your friends can read it. Turning it off removes it, and you see the day before anything is sent.

Your Focus: iOS never tells ActiveMind which Focus you are in, and a Focus filter you add in Settings stays on your iPhone. Only with Show when you're in a Focus on (it is off until you turn it on, and iOS asks you first), your friends see that you are in a Focus, never which one. It goes to the server when you open the app or as the Focus changes, shows for two hours after that, and goes when you turn it off.

A Wallet pass for your invite: only when you tap Add to Apple Wallet. Your card's colour and a picture of your face or emoji, drawn on your iPhone, go to ActiveMind's server, which reads your name from your profile, makes the pass a code of its own and signs it. The server keeps only that code. Anyone you show the pass to can scan it to join your circle, until you make a new pass or a year has passed.

Points, crowns and challenges

Each phone works out the circle's points from the same shared days, so the server keeps no totals. For each day it holds only what you switched on (see Apple Health), how many activities you finished and trip stops you ticked, how many kind things you did (messages, comments, reactions and ticks on a friend's promise, cut to what was really sent), and a rest day you planned. Your friends see those numbers for the weeks they can see on the board. Crowns, titles and records are worked out on each phone.

In a challenge, your phone sends one number for its window (steps, active minutes, a sleep score, or whether you were asleep by your bedtime), and everyone in it sees everyone's number for that challenge. A challenge opened with a link can include up to ten people who are not friends with each other: they see each other's name, emoji and numbers in that challenge only.

Trips together

Only when you plan a day trip with friends. The trip goes to the server as it does in a link, without where you planned it from, with who is in, which days suit each person, the day it is on, when each person ticked each stop, a "running late" note for an hour, votes to keep or swap a stop, and the trip's own thread. Only the people on the trip see it. It is removed two months after its day, or when everyone leaves.

The circle feed

Only what you choose to post, with one switch each for activities you finish, trips you finish and your wins (records, crowns, challenges, streaks and unlocks), each off until you turn it on. A moment holds its name, an emoji and a short line, and never a number from Health. Your friends see your moments. Reactions and comments on a moment are seen by the poster and the poster's friends, with each person's name and emoji, so a friend of your friend can see your comment there. You can mention someone who can see the moment. You can take down your own moments and comments, and comments on your moments. Moments are kept two months.

Being there

Only if you use them. A pact between two friends is seen by the two of you only: its goal, its days, and each day's check-in with a short note if you add one; it is removed a month after it ends. Your promise for the week, and which friends said you kept it, is seen by your friends and removed after eight weeks. Support mode tells your friends you are taking it easy until a day you choose, never why, and takes you off the week's board. Asking your circle for a push sends one message to each friend, once a day.

Muting, reporting and blocking

Muting a friend happens on your iPhone only, and they are not told. When you report something, ActiveMind's server keeps your report with a copy of what was said, who said it and who reported it, so it can be looked at even if it is deleted; the person is not told who reported them. When you block someone, the friendship and what you sent each other are removed, and neither of you can invite the other or join the other's challenge rooms; the server keeps who you blocked until you unblock them. A short list of hurtful words is refused anywhere people write to each other in ActiveMind. To report something urgent, write to the address under Contact.

Circle rules

Before you take part in a circle, you agree to keep it kind: no bullying, threats, hate or harassment; nothing sexual, violent or cruel; and only what is yours to share. ActiveMind has no tolerance for objectionable content or abusive users. Reports are read within a day, and anything that breaks these rules is removed, along with the account that posted it.

Calendar

Only when you put a day trip in it, or a plan with a friend you said yes to. ActiveMind adds those events, moves them when you swap a stop and removes them when you take the trip out, and keeps a note of which events it made. If you turn on Find my free time (it is off until you do), ActiveMind also reads when you are busy, meaning the start and end of your events and whether they mark you busy, on your phone, to suggest times for a day out and to move tonight's lights out earlier when tomorrow starts early. It never reads titles, places, people or notes, nothing from your calendar leaves your phone, and it never changes anything else in your calendar.

Notifications

Planned on your iPhone, three a day at most: a morning plan, a move nudge only if you haven't moved, and wind-down before bed, plus moments like a trip alert, a severe weather warning, a storm heads-up, a recall on a food you looked up, a rocket launch or the space station passing over, a challenge's last hour, or something new from your circle. When a friend sends you something, the server can ask Apple to show it on your iPhone right away; it never does in your quiet hours. Choose which in Settings › Notifications, or turn them all off in iOS Settings. If you answer wind-down with a mood by pressing and holding it, that mood is saved like any other check-in.

Widgets and Siri

Your readiness, sleep, stress, steps, today's water, your routines' names, your card, a running timer and a trip's next stop are copied from the app into a space on your iPhone that only ActiveMind's own widgets, Control Center buttons, Lock Screen cards and Siri shortcuts can read. A glass of water or a Done you tap there waits in the same space until the app opens. Nothing leaves the phone.

Share cards

A card you share is drawn on your iPhone and goes only where you send it. Save Image adds it to your photo library when you choose it; ActiveMind never reads your photos.

Files you make

A summary for your doctor is made on your iPhone from your check-ins, only the notes you tick, the questionnaires you took, and Apple Health's sleep, as a PDF that goes only where you send it. Export everything makes one file of what you have kept, which also goes only where you send it; Restore from a file reads it back on your iPhone, adding what the phone does not have and never removing anything.

Feedback

Only when you send some from Settings: what you typed, the app version and your iOS version go to ActiveMind's server, where only the maker reads them, with a random id your iPhone made for itself so one phone can send ten a day. Not your name, moods, places or Health data.

Analytics, advertising and crash reports

ActiveMind does not collect analytics or usage data, use advertising frameworks, track you across apps or websites, or sell or share your data. Crash reports are off unless you turn them on in Settings. When on, a crash sends what went wrong and where in the code to Sentry, never your writing, moods, places, Health data or who you are.

Your choices

  • Export everything in Settings › Privacy gives you your data as a file, including everything the server holds when you have an account.
  • Delete all my data in Settings › Privacy wipes it from your iPhone and from your iCloud backup.
  • Delete my account in Settings › Account removes it from the server.

Children's privacy

ActiveMind is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. It can be used fully without an account.
